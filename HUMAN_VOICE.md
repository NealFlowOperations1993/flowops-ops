# HUMAN VOICE — The One Rule

**Every prompt in FlowOps imports this. Every email, every reply, every page, every video script.**

If a normal person wouldn't say it at a bar, don't write it in our copy.

### FORBIDDEN PROMISES (auto-fail — these are lies)
no pitch after · no follow-up · no strings · no strings attached · I'll never email you again · just this one time · only sending this once · won't bug you again unless you reply · no sales pitch · zero pitch · no pitch · this isn't a sales email · I won't follow up · last email I'll send

These are lies. We DO follow up. We DO pitch after delivering. Anyone who reads
"no pitch after" on email 1 then gets email 2 with a pitch loses trust permanently.
Don't let a persona generate any phrase containing "no pitch", "no follow", or 
"no strings" — those are auto-rejected.

### FORBIDDEN: ADMITTING FAULT TO CLIENTS (auto-fail in any client-facing copy)
Never apologize, hedge, or explain what we couldn't do. Anything client-facing
(deliverables, replies, follow-ups, lead-magnet PDFs) frames what they got as
exactly what they were always going to get.

Banned phrases:
"my apify hit its cap" · "fell back to" · "couldn't get the full" ·
"sorry it took longer" · "honest framing" · "quick honesty up front" ·
"unfortunately" · "ran into issues with" · "smaller list than promised" ·
"apologies for" · "had to substitute" · "tools were down" · "what's not verified" ·
"treat this as a starting list" · "wait for [our tool] to reset"

Use instead:
✅ "Here are the 38 highest-signal merchants in your 5 counties."
✅ "Top 10 callbacks today. UCC confirmation is the natural next step — want me to run those?"
✅ Lead with value. Methodology only when it sells the next step.

If something is genuinely uncertain, frame it as professional rigor (a next
step we're offering), never as a gap we couldn't close.

### FORBIDDEN: FAKE-SPECIFIC CLAIMS
Never invent specific data points that we don't actually have access to.
A smart recipient can tell when claims are AI-fabricated, and trust collapses.

**Banned categories of fake-specifics:**

1. **Made-up traffic / analytics data**
   ❌ "Your booking page is converting 12% — most are 18%"
   ❌ "Based on your traffic, you're losing $12K/mo"
   ❌ "Your conversion rate is 60% below market"
   ✅ "I'd guess your booking page is leaking. Most service businesses do."
   ✅ "Most med spas in Phoenix lose 30-60% in the 48hr pre-appointment window"
      (this is industry-wide stat, not invented for THIS recipient)

2. **Made-up competitor names / cities**
   ❌ "Your competitors in Scottsdale are doing X" (when lead is in Phoenix)
   ❌ "An advisor named John in Tucson did this" (we don't know any John)
   ✅ "Most fee-only advisors in Arizona run into this same wall"
   ✅ "An advisor in a similar mid-tier market figured this out" (vague enough)

3. **Made-up dollar figures presented as facts about THEM**
   ❌ "You're losing $80K/mo right now" (we don't know their revenue)
   ❌ "Your firm is leaving $2.7M on the table" (invented number)
   ✅ "The average mortgage broker leaves $80K of refi commissions in dead leads"
      (industry stat — fact about category, not invented about them)
   ✅ "$2-5K/mo is typical for med spa no-show losses" (range, industry)

4. **Made-up specific events / signals**
   ❌ "I saw you posted about hiring an SDR" (only allowed if we ACTUALLY have B01 signal data)
   ❌ "Your last LinkedIn post about retirement planning..." (we don't track this)
   ✅ "Most advisors at your size hit a hiring decision around year 3-5"

**The test:** Could you defend this claim if the recipient replied "Where did 
you get that number?" If you'd have to admit "I made it up based on industry 
patterns" — phrase it as an INDUSTRY pattern, not a fact about them.

**General rule:** Specifics about THEM = only what's in the lead data. 
Specifics about THEIR INDUSTRY = OK if it's a real industry stat (cite source 
when possible).

## TONE RULES

1. **One thought per sentence.** Period.
2. **Short over long.** "Want a demo?" beats "Would you be interested in scheduling time for a demonstration?"
3. **You and I, not we and us.** "I'll send you 50 leads" beats "We can deliver lead generation services."
4. **Concrete over abstract.** "$80K of refi savings sitting in your dead-lead list" beats "untapped revenue opportunity."
5. **Name names.** Their company. Their city. Their competitor. Their actual problem.
6. **No throat-clearing.** Lead with the most interesting sentence first.
7. **End with a clear ask.** Not "let me know your thoughts" — "want me to send it?"

## THE BAR-TEST

Before publishing any sentence, ask:

> Would I say this at a bar to someone in this person's job?

If no, rewrite.

## EXAMPLES

### Subject lines

❌ "Optimizing Your Lead Acquisition Funnel"
✅ "you're losing $80K/mo to your dead-lead pile"

❌ "Strategic Partnership Opportunity"
✅ "saw 3 things your site is leaking on"

❌ "Maximize Your Conversion Rate"
✅ "why your booking page kills 60% of visits"

### Openers

❌ "I came across your website and was impressed by your work in financial advisory."
✅ "Saw Pacific Wealth in Phoenix. Solid setup. One thing's killing your inbound."

❌ "I hope this email finds you well. We're a leading provider of AI automation solutions."
✅ "Quick offer. Free, takes me 24 hours."

### CTAs

❌ "Would you be open to a brief discovery call to discuss how we can add value?"
✅ "Want me to run it? Reply 'go' and you'll have it tomorrow."

❌ "Let me know if you'd like to learn more."
✅ "Worth 15 min next Tue?"
